# Boss research: what made MMO raid bosses great, and what we can use

Research for hand-made bosses (2026-10-22). The owner asked: look at end-game raid bosses players liked in
World of Warcraft and Final Fantasy XIV (and elsewhere), and log every good mechanic we could use. Sources are
at the end. Most "best boss" lists are opinion pieces and forum threads, not polls, so the favourites below
are the names that came up again and again.

**The owner's decisions:** not a boss on every floor; bosses come at the end of an act of **at least three floors**
(so a 6-floor run is two acts: bosses after floors 3 and 6, gatekeepers on the others). The first hand-made boss,
Slumbear the Jumbled King, is in the game (r143; CLAUDE.md has the details).

## 1. The bosses players loved, and why

### World of Warcraft

| Boss | What it did | Why players loved it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lich King** (Icecrown Citadel) | Four phases over ~15 minutes. Adds early on; then the edge of the platform **crumbles away** and the fight moves to the middle. **Defile**: a dark pool under a player that **grows every time it hurts someone**. **Val'kyr** grab players and **carry them toward the edge**. A heavy tank hit forces a defensive answer. Near the end he **pulls players into his sword**, a short fight inside another world, before the final blow. | The most praised fight in the game: the villain of the whole story, a fight that keeps changing, and a finale with a story moment inside the mechanics. |
| **Lei Shen** (Throne of Thunder) | Four **conduits in the corners**. Near one, the boss gains that corner's power, and the conduit levels up. You decide **where to fight him** to control which powers grow. Intermissions shut down the strongest conduit (on Heroic it stays on for good). A tight last phase. | Top players called him "the greatest raid boss ever made". **Choosing a strategy** (which corners), and a last phase where a kill or a wipe feels close. |
| **Yogg-Saron** (Ulduar) | Three phases. Adds that **explode when killed: kill them next to the boss to hurt her**. A team goes **into the boss's mind** (a brain room) and can only hurt the brain during a short window. Everyone has **Sanity**, which drops from mechanics and comes back in green beams of light; at zero you're controlled by the boss. A **gaze you must look away from**. **Difficulty you choose**: fewer helpers = harder fight, better rewards. | Called one of the most perfectly made encounters. A strange, scary fight that tells its story through mechanics, and an optional harder version. |
| **Algalon** (Ulduar) | **Collapsing stars** die on their own and blow up; **each leaves a black hole**. His room-wide **Big Bang** kills you unless you **hide in a black hole**. The tank hit stacks a debuff, and **at 5 stacks you're thrown out of the fight**, so tanks swap. Meteors on red circles. One hour to beat him, ever. | Only reachable by beating hard modes. The hazards you dodge become the shelter you need. |
| **Mimiron** (Ulduar) | Three machines, one per phase; in the **last phase all three join into one** robot, and its parts must die **within 15 seconds of each other** or they come back. A **big red button** in the room switches on hard mode: a timer, harder phases, **flames that creep after you** and spread, and better loot. | Players loved the red button: the hard mode is a choice made in the room. |
| **Ragnaros** (Firelands) | A giant **hammer smash** that sends **lava waves** out in lines. **Seeds** burst into elementals. Sections of the floor **light up with lava** one after another. **Meteors** chase players. | Spectacle and difficulty; often named the best Heroic boss. |
| **Blackhand** (Blackrock Foundry) | Three phases, each in a **different place**: the floor **collapses** and the fight drops to a lower level, then a moving platform. Falling debris, players marked for a heavy throw, a smash that hits the whole group less the more of you stand together. | "Probably the best end boss ever": **no dull phase**. Nine minutes that stay exciting all the way. |
| **Kil'jaeden** (Sunwell Plateau) | **Dragon orbs** in the room turn a player into a dragon for a while. The dragon's **shield** is the only protection from a huge attack. Each phase adds orbs; **beat each phase before he runs out of the attacks** you can shield. | One of the game's great villains. Temporary powers handed out by the room, and racing the boss's big attack. |
| **Archimonde, Durumu, Thogar, Star Augur, Elisande** | Also named a lot: Durumu's tight time limit, Thogar's **trains** crossing the room (an arena hazard on a timetable), Star Augur's polished tuning, Elisande's **time bubbles** (fast and slow zones), Gul'dan's **secret phase** on Mythic. | Unique ideas, tight finishes, and **secret phases** for players who went further. |

### Final Fantasy XIV

| Boss | What it did | Why players loved it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Epic of Alexander** (ultimate) | Two machines fought together, a liquid boss with a hard damage check, then **Perfect Alexander**, who **shows the party a vision of the future** that they must act out later. The enrage **freezes the party in time** instead of just killing them. | Ranked the best ultimate: great music (each phase its own track), difficulty at the start not the end, mechanics easier to understand than the others', and the most creative enrage. |
| **Dragonsong's Reprise** (ultimate) | Seven phases retelling the story's dragon war **as an alternate ending**. Two dragons each **cleave half the room in opposite directions**, leaving a **safe quarter**. A lot of randomness: **phases rarely repeat**, so you can't memorise your way through. | Story, music and flashy, satisfying mechanics. **Criticised for length** (17-20 minutes). |
| **The Omega Protocol** (ultimate) | Six phases, all forms of one machine. Everyone gets a debuff that goes off later and **can only be cleared by standing in a tower**. The weakened boss fires **all its strongest attacks one after another**. | Fast, **short phases, almost no downtime**; satisfying. Criticised for being mentally exhausting and for a damage check that comes down to luck. |
| **The Unending Coil** (ultimate) | Three dragons go untargetable and **fly through the arena together**, leaving safe spots at the edges. Two bosses **must die at about the same time**, or the survivor uses its ultimate attack. | Set the "trio" formula later fights copied. Criticised for imprecise dives that feel unfair. |
| **Kefka** (O8S) | **Lying telegraphs**: an attack marked with "?" does the **opposite** of what it shows. He **records an attack and replays it later**. In his god form, triangles fill the arena and **explode in the order they appeared**. | A villain whose mechanics feel like him: the mad clown lies to you. |
| **Hephaistos** (P8S) | Puzzles where you combine buffs to solve towers, **repeats that change a little each time**, and a revive after a soft enrage. His **first form decides the timeline**, so the fight isn't always the same. | Called one of the best fights the game has had: puzzles, tight checks, a great soundtrack. |
| **Athena** (P12S) | **Chains telegraph upcoming attacks**, light and dark lasers, combined "superchain" mechanics. | Clear telegraphs that still make you think. |
| **Proto-Carbuncle** (P5S) | Crystals that **bounce off the walls**, a cage breaking for an intermission, and a **hunger bar**: feed the boss slimes or it enrages. | One of the most original mechanics in the tier. |
| **Shiva** (E8S) | **Mirrors copy her next attack**, each colour after a different delay (now, soon, later). | Remembering a sequence and dodging the echoes. |
| **Kokytos** (P9S) | Its **form tells you the next attack** (fire, ice, martial arts, meteors). | A clear way in: the boss's look tells you what's coming. |
| **Recent favourites** (Dawntrail savage) | Bosses that **teleport across platforms**, **arenas that change mid-fight**, a non-stop flow with no downtime; music and personality (Honey B. Lovely, Sugar Riot). | Energy, music and character count as much as mechanics. |

### Elsewhere

- **Destiny**: Atheon **teleports a random team into the past or future** and they must find the way back; Oryx's fight **stitches together mechanics from every earlier boss** in the raid. Players love roles, callouts and the moment a mechanic clicks.
- **Guild Wars 2**: the Vale Guardian **teaches the raid's mechanics one at a time** (it splits into three coloured guardians); a lightning cloud **must be shared by four players** or everyone takes 80% damage. The Great Jungle Wurm has **three heads that must be cut off at the same time**.
- **Lost Ark**: a **stagger bar** you must fill before a huge attack or it wipes the raid; **armour you break** with heavy hits; **counter windows** (the boss flashes, hit it now); Vykas **charms** a player into attacking their friends.
- **Hades** (an action roguelike like ours): every area ends with a boss; bosses **change between phases** (Megaera dashes, then calls helpers), you meet some bosses again in later runs, and the game **keeps surprising you**. Its optional **Extreme Measures** makes each boss harder in new ways for players who want it.

## 2. What players disliked (avoid)

- **Long fights and dull early phases.** Dragonsong's length was its main complaint, and Blackhand was praised exactly for having no dull phase. Fights where you **can't hit the boss** for long stretches (downtime) are disliked.
- **Losing control of your character.** Malygos's dragon phase and charm mechanics are named among the most hated. If we take control away, keep it to a moment.
- **Randomness you can't answer.** Unpredictable attacks that force a bad choice. Randomness should change **what** comes, never **whether** you can survive it.
- **Imprecise or unfair hits:** standing in the right spot and still getting hit (UCoB's dives). Blizzard's newest raids drop "inhuman precision" mechanics on purpose.
- **Unreadable visuals:** a fight called a "visual nightmare" despite good mechanics.
- **One mistake wipes everything:** a single misstep killing the whole group. With permadeath in our game, a mistake should cost a lot of health, not end the run outright.
- **Time sinks:** long walks back, slow intros every attempt, forced role-play. On repeat runs, intros must be short.
- **Maths puzzles and memorising long scripts.**

## 3. Design lessons

1. **Telegraph everything** and punish bad timing, not bad visuals. The game already draws crisp dashed warnings after the lighting.
2. **Teach, then combine.** Each phase introduces **one** new idea; the last phase mixes them (Vale Guardian, Omega, Oryx).
3. **Each boss has a signature mechanic** that fits its fantasy (Lei Shen powers his citadel, Kefka lies, Yogg drives you mad). One idea people remember beats ten generic ones.
4. **Short phases and no dull stretches.** Keep the player hitting something most of the time.
5. **The arena takes part:** collapsing edges, a floor that drops, trains, lava lanes, platforms.
6. **Strategy choices**, not one right answer (Lei Shen's corners, Blast Furnace's phase 1 deciding phase 3).
7. **Optional hard modes inside the fight** for better rewards (Ulduar's red button, fewer helpers).
8. **Variety on repeats:** randomised order or slightly changed repeats (Hephaistos, Dragonsong), so a roguelike boss doesn't go stale.
9. **A tight, exciting finish:** the last phase is the most desperate, and the kill or the wipe feels close.
10. **Music and story carry it:** a track per phase or an escalating track; the boss says something; a story moment inside a mechanic (the Lich King's sword, Dragonsong's alternate ending).
11. **Secret phases and hidden conditions** for players who dig (Gul'dan, Algalon).
12. **A final boss that remembers the run** (Oryx uses every earlier boss's mechanic).

## 4. The mechanics log, adapted to our game

What our game has to build on: one Coreature out at a time, the party benched in the Arcbracer, **swapping** (8 s cooldown), **types** with clear matchups, the **trainer** walking behind, auto-aimed moves, the **Restore** and **Glitch** systems, relics, the old civilization's ruins and data. Rated: ★★★ great fit, ★★ good, ★ maybe.

### Fight structure

| # | Mechanic | From | In our game | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Phases at health marks** with a transition (roar, short invulnerability, music shift) | everyone | Every boss: 2-4 phases at about 66% / 33%; the boss stops, a banner names the phase, the music changes. | ★★★ |
| 2 | **Each phase in a new place** | Blackhand, Lich King, Dawntrail | The floor cracks and the fight **drops to a lower room** (a new room picture), or the edges crumble into pits and the arena shrinks. | ★★★ |
| 3 | **Intermission with adds** | Ragnaros, Lei Shen, Megaera | The boss goes untouchable and calls helpers, or a short survival stretch; it ends when the adds fall or a timer runs out. | ★★ |
| 4 | **Combined final phase** | Mimiron's robot, UCoB's trio, Oryx | Earlier phases' forms **merge**; the final run boss uses one mechanic from each act boss you beat. | ★★★ |
| 5 | **Two bosses that must fall together** | UCoB, Mimiron, the Wurm's heads | A pair: kill one and the other powers up or revives the first, unless both go down within a few seconds. Good use of bursts and beams that hit both. | ★★ |
| 6 | **A fight inside another world** | Lich King's sword, Yogg's brain room, Atheon | Mid-fight, the boss pulls your Coreature **into its Arc-core**: a short data-space room with different rules (break memory fragments before a timer), then back out. Fits the world's story perfectly. | ★★★ |
| 7 | **Secret phase** | Gul'dan, Algalon | A hidden phase or a different final boss if a condition is met (all legends found, a special relic). Ties into the roadmap's secret final floor. | ★★ |
| 8 | **Randomised order and changed repeats** | Dragonsong, Hephaistos, Atheon | The boss picks its phase order or its first form each run; repeats of an attack change slightly. Keeps it fresh across runs. | ★★★ |

### Dodging and positioning

| # | Mechanic | From | In our game | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | **Big telegraphed hits with safe gaps** | Ragnaros's smash and lava waves, Dragonsong's opposite cleaves (safe quarter), UCoB's dives | Half-room and quarter-room attacks with a clear warning; two at once leave one safe corner; a smash that sends waves along lines. | ★★★ |
| 10 | **Hazards that grow if you feed them** | Lich King's Defile | A pool under your Coreature that **grows each time it hurts anyone**: drop it at the edge, away from where you're fighting. | ★★★ |
| 11 | **Baited puddles** | Twintania's Liquid Hells | The boss drops pools where you **were**; keep moving in a line at the room's edge so they don't cover the middle. | ★★ |
| 12 | **Spreading fire you put out with a type** | Mimiron's flames | Flames creep after you and spread; **Water moves put them out**, Wind moves blow them back. Types matter to the arena, not only to damage. | ★★★ |
| 13 | **Hide in what the boss left behind** | Algalon's black holes, Kil'jaeden's shield | A room-wide blast you survive **only inside a safe zone** made by an earlier mechanic (a dead star's hole, a broken pillar's shadow). | ★★★ |
| 14 | **Line of sight** | many | Pillars that block a beam; hide behind one. They might crumble after one use. | ★★ |
| 15 | **Knockback into hazards** | Ragnaros, Blackhand | A blast that knocks you back toward pits or lava: stand where the knockback carries you somewhere safe. | ★★ |
| 16 | **Arena timetable** | Thogar's trains | Rolling boulders, a flood or a conveyor crossing lanes on a schedule; you learn it. | ★★ |
| 17 | **Fast and slow zones** | Elisande's time bubbles | Bubbles that speed up or slow down whatever is inside, including attacks. A Metal or Arcane boss. | ★ |
| 18 | **Bouncing projectiles** | Proto-Carbuncle's crystals | Shots that bounce off the room's walls: the angles are the puzzle. | ★★ |

### Swapping and types (our own twist on tank swaps and roles)

| # | Mechanic | From | In our game | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | **A stacking mark that forces a swap** | Algalon's Phase Punch, Lei Shen's Decapitate | The boss's hits stack a mark on your Coreature; **at 5 it's stunned or knocked out of the fight**. **Swap** to reset it. Teaches swapping through the fight. | ★★★ |
| 20 | **Type phases** | Kokytos's forms, Lei Shen's corner powers | The boss **changes type** each phase, shown by its look and colour. The right counter changes, so the whole party matters. | ★★★ |
| 21 | **Positional powers** | Lei Shen's conduits | Pylons in the corners, each a type: the boss **takes that type's power while near it**. You choose where to fight; letting one pylon overcharge locks that power in for good. | ★★★ |
| 22 | **Damage to the whole party** | soaks (Vale Guardian, Omega's towers) | A **tower** that, unless your Coreature stands in it, **hurts every Coreature in the Arcbracer**, benched ones too. A new kind of pressure. | ★★★ |
| 23 | **Charm** | Vykas, Yogg's insanity | The boss **charms** your Coreature: for a few seconds it walks on its own toward a hazard. **Swap it out to save it.** Short, so it isn't a loss of control. | ★★ |
| 24 | **A meter that fills** | Yogg's Sanity, Proto-Carbuncle's hunger | A **corruption meter** (our Glitch system): some attacks raise it; full = your Coreature turns Glitched for the rest of the fight. Clear it by standing in pure light wells. | ★★★ |

### The boss itself

| # | Mechanic | From | In our game | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | **Stagger bar before a huge attack** | Lost Ark's stagger checks | The boss charges its ultimate; a bar appears; **heavy hits fill it** (big moves count more). Fill it in time and the boss collapses, open to damage; fail and the ultimate goes off. | ★★★ |
| 26 | **Counter windows** | Lost Ark | The boss **flashes blue** mid-wind-up: a hit right then interrupts it and stuns it. Rewards watching it. | ★★★ |
| 27 | **Interruptible heal** | many | A boss that eats or heals (Slumbear's Snack Time) can be **interrupted** with enough damage while it does. | ★★ |
| 28 | **Shields and breakable armour** | Kil'jaeden's shield orbs, Lost Ark's destruction | The boss is shielded by orbiting crystals; break them first. Breakable parts (Garrishell's cannons) switch off their attacks. | ★★ |
| 29 | **Use its helpers against it** | Yogg's exploding guardians, Ragnaros's seeds | Adds that explode when they fall: **kill them next to the boss** to break its shield or hurt it. Turns hazards into tools. | ★★★ |
| 30 | **Reflect phase** (our answer to "look away") | Yogg's gaze | A mirror shell that **reflects your attacks**: stop attacking and dodge until it cracks. Auto-aim makes this readable: your move buttons grey out with a warning. | ★★ |
| 31 | **Recorded attacks played back** | Kefka's Mana Charge, Shiva's mirrors | The boss **records an attack and replays it later**, together with a new one. Fits the Archive perfectly: it replays recorded data. Mirrors echo its attack now, soon, later. | ★★★ |
| 32 | **A vision of the future** | Perfect Alexander, Atheon | The boss shows a ghostly preview of its next sequence; it plays it out a few seconds later. You plan your path from the vision. | ★★ |
| 33 | **Lying telegraphs** | Kefka's "?" attacks | A **Glitch** boss whose corrupted warnings sometimes mean the opposite, marked with a glitch symbol. Use rarely and fairly. | ★★ |
| 34 | **Temporary powers from the room** | Kil'jaeden's dragon orbs, Thorim's storm | Ancient **Arc-core pylons**: step on one and your Coreature gains a power or shield for a short time; or the **trainer** activates them with the Arcbracer. | ★★★ |
| 35 | **Adds that go for the trainer** | Lich King's Val'kyr | Helpers that **grab your trainer and drag them toward the edge**; knock the helpers down before the trainer is carried off. A downed trainer could cost a big hit or lock swapping for a while. A new idea for our game. | ★★★ |

## 5. How bosses fit a run (the owner: not a boss every floor)

What other roguelikes do: **Slay the Spire** has 3 acts with a boss at the end of each and elites in between.
**Hades** ends each area with a boss and has mini-bosses inside. **Dead Cells** puts a boss after every 2-3
areas. **Isaac** has bosses every floor but groups floors into chapters.

Options for our 6 floors:
- **A. Three acts of two floors**, a boss at the end of floors 2, 4 and 6 (6 = the final boss). The other floors
  end with a **gatekeeper**: a tough Coreature or an elite room that opens the stairs, like today's bosses.
  Each act has 2-3 possible bosses, so about **9 bosses plus the final one** cover full runs with variety.
  Endless keeps a boss every 2 floors.
- **B. Two acts of three floors**, bosses at 3 and 6. Fewer, bigger fights; longer stretches without a big moment.

Claude's suggestion: **A**. Three bosses per run is the Slay the Spire and Hades rhythm, the bosses can be bigger
and better than if we needed one per floor, and the gatekeepers keep the other floors from ending flat.
