# Coreatures: Deep Archive

(Called **Monster Rush** until r80; the owner renamed it on 2026-10-20. The repo and Pages link still say `monster-rush` until the owner renames the repo on GitHub.)

A real-time, top-down pixel-art **roguelike** in the style of *The Binding of Isaac*, built on the combat engine of the owner's arena game, Monster Battle Stadium (`mendezm87/monster-battle-stadium`, live at https://mendezm87.github.io/monster-battle-stadium/). The owner likes how that game plays: **keep its combat and expand it for dungeons.** Plain HTML5 Canvas + vanilla JS: no build step, no asset files, no dependencies. Scripts load as classic `<script>` tags in `index.html` (globals, not ES modules), so the page also works from `file://`. Runs on phone (touch), PC (mouse + keyboard) and gamepads.

## Names (decided with the owner, 2026-10-19/20)

- **The game:** *Coreatures: Deep Archive* (logo: COREATURES over DEEP ARCHIVE; short form "Deep Archive" in page titles and comments). Monster Battle Stadium keeps its own name.
- **The creatures:** **Coreatures** in everything this game shows players and in this game's own comments. Code names shared with the Stadium stay as they are there (`MONSTER_ART`, `js/monsters/`, `Mon`, `monSprite`), and the shared files (sprites.js, monsters, fx.js, audio.js, describe.js, movesets.js) say "monster", because they're monsters in the Stadium.
- **The crystals:** **Arc-cores** (Archive Cores); the HUD's party row is the Arcbracer's slots (`.slots`). **XP pickups:** **Arc-core Shards** (`SHARD_IMG`, pickup `shard`). **The bracelet:** the **Arcbracer** (the phone is its screen). **Deploying** replaced throwing balls (`this.deploys`; the click sound for swaps and move changes is `blip`, `ball` stays for the Stadium).
- **Trainer looks:** `TRAINER_LOOK_NAMES` (every look's display name), `TRAINER_DEFAULT_LOOK`, `Battle.look`.

## Workflow

- **Always push after committing** unless the owner says otherwise for that piece of work; no need to ask first, since changes can be rolled back.
- **Work directly on `main`.** The owner doesn't want a clutter of branches; only make a branch if they ask for one, and merge and delete it when done.
- **Bump `VERSION` in `js/main.js` with every change you push** (format `YYYY.MM.DD-rN`). It shows on the title screen so the owner can check a device isn't running a cached older build (GitHub Pages lets browsers cache files for up to 10 minutes).
- **Always test with the sound off.** Right after every page load, run `if (!Sfx.muted) toggleMute()` (mute resets on reload).
- The owner is a hobbyist who prefers plain-language explanations and being shown results; they decide design, Claude proposes and builds.

## The design (decided with the owner)

- **Structure like The Binding of Isaac.** Each floor is a grid of rooms connected by doors. Entering a room with monsters **locks the doors until it's cleared**. A floor has a start room, normal fight rooms, a **boss room** (beat it to open the way down), a **treasure room**, a **shop**, and **recruit rooms**. Clear floors to go deeper.
- **Combat = the arena game's combat, expanded.** Real-time, auto-aimed moves, types, statuses, beams, the newer move kinds, mid-fight swapping. The big expansion: **rooms hold 1–3 wild monsters at once**, all fighting your one active monster (done: auto-aim targets the nearest wild monster, and beams, bursts, zones, vortexes and pools hit every wild monster they touch). Some special rooms hold a single tougher monster.
- **Your team.** Pick **a starter** at the beginning of a run and **recruit** along the way. **Recruit rooms offer a choice of monsters (e.g. pick 1 of 3).** The party has a size limit (6, like now); the player must be able to **release a monster or swap one out** for a newly offered one. One monster is active at a time; you can swap between party members as now.
- **Exploring:** the player controls their active monster directly; **their trainer character walks behind, following them.**
- **Trainer look (r74, hand-shaped, owner: "do it" after comparing a sample):** four trainers (`TRAINERS`) × him and her, drawn from the owner's concept art (`docs/concept-prompts.md` T1-T4, T1F-T4F) in `js/trainers.js` (loaded right after pixel.js on every page that shows a trainer). Pictures are 34x42 (the figure ~20 wide, the rest is room for the Scarf Tamer's flying scarf or a coat tail), drawn in body coordinates x 0-23 (`TOX` added). Each look in `TRAINER_LOOKS` has typed `heads` (front, side, back; 20 wide; `lash` = where her lashes go) and a `body(P, v, f)` of hand-picked rows and pixels with hand-picked shades (light from the top-left); fside is the front head with the face slid one pixel right (`turnHead`), bside the back head with a cheek peeking out (`peekHead`). Arms are separate (3 wide): `TRAINER_ARMS` (where they hang), each look's `arm(k)` (colours from shoulder to hand, R wears the Arcbracer, `1` band and `2` screen), `trainerArmPx` lays them at any angle, so the pose rig in trainer.js swings them. Rows: head 0-16, body 17-29, legs 30-41 (`TR_BODY`, `TR_LEGS`), a walking leg lifts the boot rows (`TR_FEET`, split at `TR_SPLIT`). Mouths: `K` lips, `3` teeth (an open-mouth pose darkens both). **Faces (r75, owner: "they all look a little nervous", some smiles "creepy"):** high flat brows and wide eyes with whites read as worried, and a wide bar of teeth as a grimace. Faces now use brows angled down toward the middle sitting right on the eyes, a heavy upper lid with the iris tucked under the inner corner (no whites), and a lopsided smirk (a short lip line with one corner raised, no teeth). Keep them confident. **Eyes (r77, owner: "you can't really see the eyes", side views looked squinting):** every eye is 2 wide and 3 tall: the dark lid, then a white shine and the dark iris, then iris (side views: lid, then shine and iris). There's no nose pixel (it made the smirk look like a diagonal line); the mouth is a 3-pixel line with its right corner raised a row. At this sprite size that's about the limit of facial detail; more would need a bigger sprite, and a bigger trainer than monsters only works if everything moves to that scale together. In side views the near arm is inked only on its front edge and hand (inked on both edges it read as a stick). **Posed arms (r76, owner: "looks like it's attached with a stick figure arm"):** `trainerArmPx` draws an arm as a thick limb: every pixel within 1.45 of the line from the shoulder, coloured by how far along it is (the outer/middle/inner keys of `arm.cols` follow the arm at any angle), a rounded shoulder joint that meets the body, and a 3-wide hand. Posed arms are inked on empty pixels and, past the shoulder, also where they cross the body or face. Scarf Tamer: wild dark hair with a red streak, brass goggles, the long red scarf, patched charcoal bomber, crystal charms on the belt, olive cargo shorts, leggings, boots (her: long locks and a high ponytail). Field Researcher: bucket hat with red band, round glasses, khaki field coat, red neckerchief, satchel with a glowing device, rolled trousers (her: a bob). Dungeon Diver: crimson hood and torn coat, steel plate, rope coil, gauntlet with glowing studs, lantern, tall strapped boots, a plaster on the cheek (her: a braid). Shiba Trainer: shiba-eared ginger hood over ginger hair, cream hoodie with ginger raglan sleeves and a curl on the back, bum bag, black track pants with a red stripe, cream sneakers (her: pigtails). Review: `trainer.html` (all 8 in every view, walking, and every pose). Quick previews without a browser: dump `trainerMap` with Node (it needs no canvas) and paint the keys with Python/PIL. Never the famous cap-wearing trainer's exact look or logo.
- **Currency and items, picked up along the way.** Coins drop from fights and rooms and are spent in the shop. Items/relics change how moves work (ideas: "beams also drain", "dashes leave fire", stat ups, healing items, revives).
- **Growth within a run:** monsters **level up** (stats grow).
- **Evolution / transformation:** **some monsters transform** at a level, or with a special item, gaining a new look and new or upgraded moves.
- **Between runs:** unlocks (new starters, monsters, items) are **saved in the browser** (localStorage, wrapped in try/catch) and kept across runs.
- **Permadeath:** if your whole party faints, the run ends. Unlocks stay.

### Decided for the first playable (2026-10-17)

- **Wide screen, and rooms may be bigger than the screen** (Hades-style scrolling). The view is 576x324 world units (canvas 2x); rooms are 576x324, 864x324, 576x486, 864x486, and 864x540 for bosses. Phones are played sideways (a "turn your phone" notice shows in portrait).
- **3 floors per run** at first (now 6, see "A full-length run" below); an **endless mode** is wanted later, so floors keep generating from a floor number.
- **Starters:** Smugshi, Cinderox, Tidalisk (`STARTERS`); unlocks can add more.
- **Fainted monsters stay fainted until the next floor**, then get back up at 30% HP (`REVIVE_HP`). Stricter once revives are in the shop.
- **Recruit rooms** offer 3 random monsters not in your party; walk onto a pedestal to see the card. With a full party you pick who to let go. Release also lives in pause > Party (not the monster that's out mid-fight).
- **Berries** (Claude's addition, like Isaac's hearts): 30% of wild monsters drop one, healing 25% of the active monster. Needed because monsters without a heal move otherwise wear down across rooms.
- **Wild monsters are scaled-down arena monsters** (`wildSpec` in `js/dungeon.js`: about 30% HP and 36% attack on floor 1, growing per floor); "tough" single-monster rooms and bosses (bigger sprite, big HP bar, barely knocked back) sit in between. They take turns attacking (`enemyAtkT`). Balance at this point: a hard bot reaches floor 2-3 most runs and clears all 3 about 1 run in 18.
- The 2-player and online modes were removed (`js/net.js` deleted; it's in git history and in the arena repo).

### Decided in the second pass (2026-10-17)

- **Monsters and the trainer turn to face where they go:** every design has `front`, `side` (facing right, flipped for left) and `back` views (`MONSTER_ART[id](grid, view)`). A new monster needs all three, plus the two diagonals in `MONSTER_ART_DIAG` (see the third pass).
- **Leveling:** XP per wild monster beaten (`XP_WILD`/`XP_TOUGH`/`XP_BOSS`), full for the active monster, half for the rest; each level +3.5% HP, +2% attack/defence, heals 5%. Recruits join a level below the party's average.
- **Learning moves** (owner's request, 2026-10-17): every 2nd level (`LEARN_EVERY`) a monster learns a random move from its pool (`movePool` in `js/movesets.js`: its own moves plus a few coverage types, minus signature moves and moves only an evolved form has). `RunState.learnQueue` holds them and a pop-up (`#screen-learn`, `openLearn`) opens once you're out of a fight: put it in place of a slot, or keep it for later. A monster remembers every move it has known (`Mon.known`), so in pause > Party you can swap any of them back in and drag moves between buttons (tap a move, then a slot) to choose which click fires what. Each slot is labelled with its click and key. `Mon.equip(slot, id)` refuses a set with no attack at all. Recruits learn the moves for the levels they join at. On evolution, moves the evolved form upgrades change in their slots (Flamethrower -> Inferno Beam) and custom picks stay. `js/movesets.js` is **shared with Monster Battle Stadium** (custom move sets there): keep both copies identical.
- **Evolution:** the three starters evolve at level 6 (`EVOLVE_LEVEL`) once a room is cleared: Smugshi -> **Smugshogun** (grown-up Toshi: neck ruff, samurai headband, red cape, still smug), Cinderox -> **Infernox** (fire/ground magma bull: blazing mane, curved horns, lava-cracked rock shoulders, nose ring, glowing eyes), Tidalisk -> **Maelstrisk** (water/dragon sea dragon: coil, teal fin-wings, gold horns, whiskers). Each has its own hand-drawn 44px art in all three views (the owner asked for all-new art, not scaled-up starters). So far only the starters evolve. **More monsters can evolve, but not every monster needs to** (the owner's words); designs get worked out with the owner as we go.
- **Every floor has a shop and a treasure room** (dead ends, like the boss and recruit rooms). The shop sells a Big Berry, a Picnic Basket or Revive Seed, and two relics; the treasure room has one free relic.
- **Relics** (`RELICS` in `js/dungeon.js`) last the run and help the whole party: Leech Lens (beams drain), Ember Tracks (dashes leave fire), Muscle Charm, Iron Shell, Swift Boots, Hourglass, Lucky Coin, Berry Basket, Split Shot, Static Charm, Blast Core.
- **Saved unlocks** (`Save`, localStorage key `deepArchive.save.v1`): beating a monster as a boss unlocks it as a starter; runs, wins and best floor show on the title screen.
- Cinderox got more HP/attack/defence here (1200 / 1.15 / 1.1) and a bigger, quicker Eruption (r 74, delay 0.42, power 300, cd 7), because it has no heal and wild monsters kept stepping out of Eruption; the arena game's numbers are separate.
- **Endless mode** (title screen button, `RunState.endless`): the boss always opens stairs, floors keep scaling (`wildSpec` is linear in the floor number; from floor 4 rooms hold more monsters and more "tough" ones), floor themes loop through `FLOOR_STAGES` (meadow, crater, summit, then Crystal Grotto and Sunken Ruins). Once every relic is owned, treasure rooms give a picnic. The best endless floor is saved (`Save.data.bestEndless`). Bots reach floor 4-6.
- Floors have 11-15 rooms (`target` in `tryFloor`), 4 of them special (recruit, shop, treasure, boss). Level n needs `18 + 11n` XP.
- Balance at this point: the hard bot nearly always clears floor 1 and wins a bit over half its runs (Smugshi most, Cinderox least); most losses come on floor 2.

### Decided in the third pass (2026-10-17, now developed on the owner's Windows PC)

- **Diagonal views (8-way turning)**, asked for by the owner: `fside` (three-quarter front, heading down-right) and `bside` (three-quarter back, heading up-right), both flipped by `face` for the left. They live in `MONSTER_ART_DIAG` in `js/sprites.js` (sets `SPRITES[id].diag`) and `TRAINER_FSIDE`/`TRAINER_BSIDE` in `js/pixel.js`; `viewFor()` picks the view from 8 directions with a little hysteresis (or 4 for a monster without diagonals). **Every monster, the evolved starters and the trainer have them** (the owner approved the first batch, then asked for the rest). The usual recipe: from the front view, shift the face and muzzle 2-4 px toward the right, shrink the far-side ear/arm/eye a little, and move the tail out to the left; for `bside`, take the back view, shift the head right and let a sliver of muzzle or cheek peek out on the right edge. The gallery shows the diagonals and spins through all 8.
- **Everyone rigged** (r41, owner: "yes" to rigging the rest after approving Toshi): all 23 monsters animate with real frames. Body types (`RIGGED`): quad (Smugshi, Cinderox, Thornback, Frostling, Shadefang, Lumawool, Smugshogun, Infernox), biped (Brawlix, Sparkit, Slumbear, Boulderon, Mindra, Scarablade, Gearhulk, Mondaze), floater (Wispurr), flier (Drakorn, Galewing, Pixibelle: floater frames that hover; buildSprites also draws every frame wings-down, linked as `views.flapped` and swapped in by drawMon), slither (Tidalisk, Venomire, Maelstrisk: the coil or slime base is drawn on `g` and stays put while the body sways). Helpers: `rigParts(g, v)` (b, h, t, earA/B, legA/B, armA/B), `rigEye` / `rigEyes` (blink, hurt, sleep, faint eyes), `rigMouth` (open, roar), and `part()` grew `eyes()`. Per-monster choices: horns, leaves, antennae and spikes act as ears (Boulderon's face moves on his body); headband tails, Smugshogun's cape and Cinderox's flank flames ride the tail; Venomire's spots go with whichever they sit on (body or base). **Rest poses are pixel-identical to the art before rigging** (checked by comparing every view and flap frame against commit 0eb18ea; keep doing that when touching rigged art). The trainer walks in 4 frames (`trainerWalk` in pixel.js: one leg's half of the bottom three rows lifts a pixel). Workflow used: rewrite a monster's MAIN and DIAG functions with every shape on its part, swap them in, compare, then look at a frame sheet.
- **Rig upgrade before rigging everyone** (r40, the owner approved Toshi and said "let's do it" to: move poses, a proper faint, follow-through, body-type templates, better review tools). `RIGGED` maps a monster to its body type in `RIG_FRAMES`: `quad` (Toshi), `biped` (Brawlix; for Slumbear, Mondaze, Gearhulk, the trainer), `floater` (Wispurr; for Mindra). Still to define when we get there: fliers (Galewing, Pixibelle, Drakorn: fold the wing flap into the rig) and a slithering body (sea-dragon types). Rig params: body, head, tail (also trailing things like Brawlix's headband tails), legs, arms (swing forward: dx in side views, dy facing you), ears (2 = pinned back), eyes (blink / hurt / sleep / faint, drawn by `rigEye`), mouth (open / roar). Frames: 4-frame walk with the head and tail a frame behind the body (`lagged`), 4-frame idle, blink, ear flick (every ~5 s), wind-up, attack, hurt, a 2-frame run for dashes, roar for beams, throw for shots and strikes from above, proud for buffs, sleep, chew (2 frames), and faint, which `lying()` turns a quarter onto its side (exact, crisp). Casts now say what they are (`action.pose`: throw / proud / heal, plus `action.mv`; the Stadium sends `po` to online guests). Heals pose for as long as their effect plays (`HEAL_POSE`, `animHeal`): Blanket Nap sleeps, Snack Time chews, Photosynth and Wish look proud. `anim.html` has a button per pose, a speed slider, pause and step (it runs its own clock through `ANIM_CLOCK`), and a frame sheet.
- **Rigged animation** (r38, owner: "they get stuck and stop animating sometimes... this looks sort of low budget"): squashing whole sprites looked cheap, so animation now moves *parts* with real frames. A rigged monster's art draws each part through `part(g, dx, dy)` (body, head and everything on it, tail, the two diagonal pairs of legs) and reads `rig()` for where they go plus `eyes` ('blink', 'hurt') and `mouth` ('open'); with no pose set it draws exactly as before. `buildSprites` draws every frame in `RIG_FRAMES` for monsters in `RIGGED` (`SPRITES[id].frames`): a 4-frame walk (a diagonal pair lifts while the body rises, the head follows a frame late, the tail swings; a frame every 9 world units), a 4-frame idle (head bob, tail wag, 2.5 fps), blinks every ~3.7 s, wind-up, attack (mouth open, head forward) and hurt (eyes squeezed). `monAnim(m, phase, flying)` picks the frame (or, for monsters not rigged yet, just a 3-frame hop and a one-pixel dip on attacks; the squash and lean are gone except a small one when fainting or hurt). **Only Toshi is rigged so far**, for the owner to approve; the other 22 and the trainer still need rigging (route their parts through `part()`). The freeze: monsters the game pauses (wild monsters while you pick after a faint, the Stadium's pick screen) kept their speed, so they froze mid-step; walking now means `walkT` actually changing, and idle, blinks and the walk hold run on the real clock. `anim.html` (not linked) plays any monster's idle / walk / attack / hurt at real speed in all five views. **r39 fix** (owner: Toshi stuck with a sleeping face after Blanket Nap): the hurt and pop-in timers only counted down during room transitions, so after the first hit a rigged monster kept its eyes-shut hurt face all fight. Moments are now time stamps on the real clock (`animMark(m, 'hurt' | 'pop')`, `animSince`), so nothing depends on which update path runs; don't go back to countdowns for animation.
- **Animation poses for every monster** (superseded by the rig below; `posed` is still used for small dips) (r36, owner: "better animations for all the characters"; step 1 of 2): `posed(canvas, sq, sh)` in `js/sprites.js` bends a sprite at its own resolution so pixels stay crisp: `sq > 0` squashes (rows taken out of the body band, 55-85% of the height so faces stay intact, and the body widened by repeating the middle column), `sq < 0` stretches (those rows doubled), `sh` leans the top forward (negative = back). `monPose(m, ...)` picks the pose: a bouncy walk (squash as a foot lands, stretch at the top, lean when heading sideways), breathing when idle, a crouch then a stretch for casts and blasts, braced while a beam charges and pushed back while it fires, stretched and leaning into dashes, squished and knocked back when hit (`m.hurtT`, set in `hit`), popping out tall when sent in (`m.popT`), and toppling when fainting. `tintOf(canvas, colour)` tints the bent shape (hit flash, statuses). The trainer gets the bouncy step too. Shared with the Stadium (its online guest starts the hit pose from the hit flash). **r37, after the owner said r36 looked very jittery:** poses must change at a hand-animated pace, never every frame. The walk was a bounce every 7 world units (about 15 a second, 40+ shape changes a second); it's now a 3-frame hop every 30 units (land squashed, spring up, come down; about 8 changes a second), held 0.2 s after stopping so stop-start AI movement doesn't flicker, the trainer's step likewise (every 20 units); the hit pose only on real hits (`mult >= 1`, not beam/burn ticks) and not restarted while playing; wing flaps slowed (Galewing 4, Pixibelle 6, Drakorn 3 beats a second). There's no frame cap: the loop runs at the screen's refresh rate with a variable dt. **Step 2, not done yet:** hand-drawn walk cycles with moving legs, starting with Toshi for the owner to approve.
- **Flying monsters fly** (r35, owner: "make the flying monsters look like they are flying"): `FLYERS` in `js/sprites.js` lists who hovers and how fast they flap (Galewing, Pixibelle, Drakorn; Wispurr the ghost floats without flapping). `drawMon` lifts them off their shadow (smaller shadow, a slow bob, health bar raised with them), swaps between the normal frame and a wings-down frame (`SPRITES[id].flap`, built with `FLAP = 1`; faster while moving), and drops them to the ground when frozen or fainted. Wings are drawn through `wingPoly`/`wingPolyM` (swing down round their first point, or `w.root`/`w.swing`) and `wingEllipse`/`wingEllipseM` (butterfly wings fold in), so a new winged monster only needs its wing shapes routed through those plus a `FLYERS` entry. Wing feather dots are skipped in the flap frame (`FLAP ||`). Shared with the Stadium (its `drawMon` in battle.js has the same hover code).
- **More music** (the owner: "we only have one song"): `Sfx.music` holds several original songs (`SONGS` in `js/audio.js`; melodies written as note names, `mel('E5.2 G5.4 r.2')`). Rush (floor 1 meadow, the original), Magma March (floor 2 crater, D minor 160), Summit Wind (floor 3 summit, A minor 150, square lead), Deep Echoes (floors 4-5, grotto and ruins, B minor 138, half-time), Last Ascent (floor 6, spire), Boss Rush (boss rooms until the boss falls, C minor 184), Coin Corner (the shop, F major 112). `roomSong()` in `js/dungeon.js` picks one per room; `Sfx.music.play(name)` crossfades and ignores the song already playing. Levels were checked with `_scheduleAll(seconds, name)`: peaks 0.16-0.20, similar RMS.

### A full-length run (r33, owner: "add more levels to the run so it feels like a complete game")

- **6 floors per run** (`RUN_FLOORS`), one area each, in order (`FLOOR_STAGES`): Verdant Meadow, Ember Crater, Snowy Summit, Crystal Grotto, Sunken Ruins (the last two used to be endless-only), and the new **Starlit Spire** (floor 6: the top of a tower at night, indigo stone with gold trim, falling-star weather `stars`, and its own song, **Last Ascent**, `spire`: E minor 168, 16th hats, a climbing square lead).
- **The final boss** (floor 6's boss room, `wildSpec(..., 'final')`, `Mon.final`) is one of the evolved starters whose base form isn't in your party (Smugshogun, Infernox or Maelstrisk), bigger and tougher than other bosses. "FINAL BOSS!" banner, "FINAL BOSS · NAME" on the boss bar. Evolved monsters can't be unlocked as starters, so beating it unlocks nothing (yet).
- Floors start with a two-line banner: "FLOOR 2/6" (or "FINAL FLOOR") over the area's name (`floorBanner`, `UI.banner(text, sub)`); the HUD says "FLOOR 2/6" (`floorLabel`); the title shows the best floor out of 6, the results the floor and area reached.
- **Difficulty is spread over the 6 floors** (`wildSpec` grows about half as fast per floor as in the 3-floor version), so floor 6 is roughly where floor 3-4 used to be, on top of the extra levels, relics and moves a party gathers. Hard bots (r33, 8 runs each): Smugshi 3/8, Cinderox 2/8, Tidalisk 0/8 wins; losses spread over all floors; a win takes 25-35 minutes of game time. Endless uses the same curve, so it goes deeper (bots reach floor 10 in about 40 minutes).

### Every run different (r34, owner: "the runs should be different every time", after we compared Isaac and Hades)

The owner liked the whole list of ideas (in order): area monsters, door rewards, champions, relics as 1-of-3 picks with more relics and type sets, a risk-or-reward shrine per floor, floor twists (dark floor, stampede, rain), alternate areas per floor, special rooms (challenge, secret, move tutor, rival trainer), mystery treats, a Heat-style challenge mode and a daily seeded run. Done so far:
- **Area monsters** (`AREA_MONS` in `js/dungeon.js`): each area has its natives (wild monsters, `AREA_ODDS` = 75% of picks; the rest from anywhere), its own boss list (80%), and two of the three recruits come from it.
- **Door rewards** (like Hades): every fight room has a `reward` (`ROOM_REWARDS`: coins, berries, an Arc-core Shard (XP), move disc, rare treasure chest = a relic), shown as a sign inside each doorway that leads to it (`doorSign`, `drawDoorSigns`; special rooms show theirs too: skull = boss, coin = shop, chest = treasure, ball = recruit). It pops out when the room is cleared (`spawnReward`). Wild monsters drop fewer coins and berries to make up for it, so total loot is about the same; the doors decide what kind. A move disc teaches the monster that's out a move (the learn pop-up); XP candy gives the party XP.
- **Champions** (like Isaac's, `CHAMPIONS`, `makeChampion`, 9.5% of wild monsters on floor 1 rising to 27% on floor 6, capped at 30%): Swift (+40% speed, moves recharge 25% faster), Giant (bigger, 1.8x HP, half knockback, tough XP), Spiky (touching it hurts, `SPIKE_MV`, `updateChampions`), Splitting (two small copies when it faints, `splitChampion`), Shiny (rare, golden sparkles, 10 coins and a chest). A coloured ring, glow and label show the trait.
- Balance (hard bots, 8 runs each, r34): Smugshi 6/8, Cinderox 4/8, Tidalisk 3/8. Bots clear every room; with rewards switched off Smugshi was 4/8, so rewards (healing above all) are the strongest lever. Tune `ROOM_REWARDS` and the wild drops in `dropLoot` together.

- `js/fx.js` draws every attack into `FXLayer`, a pixel buffer where **one pixel = one sprite pixel** (1.5 world units). Shapes get a 1-pixel ink outline (`FXD.ink`), soft edges are snapped to hard pixels, and the layer is scaled up without smoothing after the lighting, so attacks stay crisp pixel art that matches the monsters (no glow or bloom, as the owner prefers).

### Built to grow (r58, before scaling up to Isaac-sized content; the owner: "are we ok to proceed how we have been?")

The way of working stays (vanilla JS, no build step, art drawn in code, the owner's generated concept art, bot tests). Four things were changed so it holds up at 100+ monsters and relics:
- **Monsters are drawn when first needed**, not all at start-up (30 monsters' every pose was ~3,450 pictures and 2 s on the dev PC at load, growing with every monster). `SPRITES` is a Proxy over `SPRITE_STORE`: `SPRITES[id]` draws that monster on first use, so old code works unchanged. `warmSprites(ids)` draws upcoming monsters in the background, about 4 ms at a time between frames (`spriteSteps` is a generator: one set of views per step; the rig pose and flap are set and cleared inside each step, never left on between steps). `RunState.warm()` queues a floor's monsters (every room's enemies, the recruit offers, the party and what it evolves into) on each new floor; a recruit queues its evolution. `buildSprites()` is now empty (kept so pages that call it still work). `Mon` takes its height from `def.size` and its diagonals from `MONSTER_ART_DIAG`, so making a monster doesn't draw it.
- **One art file per monster family** in `js/monsters/` (e.g. `garrishell.js` holds Shellmet, Bulwarden and Garrishell; `smugshi.js` holds Smugshogun too). Each file adds to `MONSTER_ART`, `MONSTER_ART_DIAG` and `RIGGED`. `js/monsters.js` lists the files (`MONSTER_FILES`) and loads them right after sprites.js (document.write, so it works from file:// with no build step). `js/sprites.js` keeps only the shared helpers (PixGrid wrappers, wings, rig, poses, building). **Adding a monster:** a file in js/monsters/ (or a new function in its family's file) with its MAIN, DIAG and RIGGED entries, its name in `MONSTER_FILES`, a ROSTER entry in data.js, and a place in `AREA_MONS` (or an evolution leading to it). ### Phone memory (r67, owner: on phones the player monster, the map or monsters sometimes vanished)

Mobile browsers (iPhone Safari above all) cap the memory all canvases may use and free thrown-away canvases late (the JS garbage collector doesn't see their pixel memory); once the cap is hit, **new canvases silently come out blank**, so a freshly drawn monster or room is invisible. The game was making garbage canvases constantly: `numberCanvas` made a new picture every frame for the coin counter (and per shop price tag), and every room made its own trainer pictures. Fixes: **number pictures are cached** (`_numbers`, the 400 most recent), **trainer pictures are made once** (`_trainer`), and things are **freed explicitly** with `freeCanvas(c)` (pixel.js: width and height to 0) instead of waiting for collection: the old floor's room pictures and scorch marks on each new floor, dressed gear sets nobody wears any more (`forgetGear`, gear.js; sets track their own canvases in `own`, the faint frames are shared with the plain set and never freed there), and the pictures of monsters not in the party or on the new floor (`forgetSprites(keep)` in sprites.js, called from `RunState.warm`; `SPRITES[id]` draws them again if they turn up). Measured: about 0 new canvases per frame now (it was 1-3), and the live set stays bounded (~4,400 small canvases, ~22 MB, at the end of a run). **Rule: never create a canvas per frame; cache it, and free big ones you're done with.** Not yet checked on the owner's phone.

## Rules carried over from the arena game (keep these)

- **Art style: pixel art at 32px (44px for big monsters).** The owner chose pixel art over a smooth cartoon style (2026-10-17). A 64px "HD" Toshi was tried (`art-test.html`) but looked wrong next to everything else at 32px; HD only works if the whole game (monsters, trainer, rooms, effects) moves to it together, which would be a big job to agree with the owner first.
- **Rooms are lit "HD-2D" style, attacks stay pixel art** (owner's choice, 2026-10-17, after trying `hd2d-test.html`): `js/lighting.js` adds each floor theme's mood light (`STAGES[key].light`), wall torches, soft shadows, a lens blur on the top and bottom edges, floating motes, a tint and a vignette. Everything that's an attack or a warning (beams, shots, particles, zone falls, the dashed warning outlines), plus numbers, bars and the minimap, is drawn after the lighting so it stays crisp and bright. No bloom or glow on attacks (the owner preferred them crisp). Lighting can be switched off (title screen and pause menu, saved as `Save.data.lighting`). **Performance (r42, the owner saw the frame rate drop with lighting on):** when lighting is on and `RES > 2`, `render` draws and lights the room and everyone in it in a 2x buffer (`sceneBuffer`; the art's own scale, so nothing is lost) and stretches it onto the canvas in one crisp step, so the full-screen lighting passes cost the same at 4K as at 720p; attacks, numbers and the HUD still draw at full resolution after. Inside `js/lighting.js`: the light map is half the view's size and built from pre-drawn glow sprites (no gradients per frame), the lens blur only processes the top and bottom strips by shrinking and stretching them (the canvas `blur()` filter was the slowest part), and motes, tint and vignette are pre-drawn (tint + vignette in one layer per tint). Measured at 1080p: lighting's extra cost per frame went from about 2.3 ms to 1.2 ms on the dev PC; much more on screens with more pixels or weaker graphics. Keep per-frame full-screen passes and canvas filters out of the lighting. **r43, owner: "the lighting makes everything look washed out":** measured, lit frames kept their brightness but lost about a fifth of their colour saturation (the soft-light pass at 0.5 lifts the darks and greys everything). Now `Lighting.tune` = soft 0.25 plus a `pop` pass (the lit scene overlaid on itself at 0.4), which brings saturation back to about the unlit level with the mood light kept. Check saturation numbers, not just looks, when retuning.
- **Original IP only.** Monsters, names and sprites are original designs, never Pokémon, Nintendo, Palworld or other games' assets. "Like X from game Y" requests become an original monster with a similar vibe (e.g. Mondaze, a burnt-out coffee sloth, answered "something like Palworld's Depresso"). Avoid names ending in "-mon" or using "pocket".
- **Smugshi is the owner's real dog, Toshi** (a Shiba Inu, drawn from the owner's photos: a soft ginger coat, not bright orange; lots of white: a big bib down the chest and belly, white legs, white backs of the thighs, white cheeks rising right up under the eyes; white that runs unbroken from the cheeks down the throat into the chest (no outline under the chin: an outline there read as a double chin), joined to a white sash that runs from the chest up behind the cheek and over the top of the neck (a crescent across the shoulders seen from above or behind); white on the belly, flanks and backs of the thighs; a very fluffy, mostly cream tail curl; big wide-set upright ears with pink insides edged in white fur; pale eyebrow spots; upright triangular ears with pale insides; a big black nose; a flat, unimpressed mouth; a curled tail, ginger on top with white fluff; slim and fox-like from the side; and a famously smug half-closed stare). Colours live in `TOSHI` in `js/sprites.js`. Keep his look and personality: Zoomies, Shiba Scream (its own synthesized "AAAAAH" sound, `shibaScream` in `js/audio.js`, picked via the move's `sfx` field), Smug Beam, Blanket Nap. A natural fit as a starter.
- **Music is an original chiptune** (Game Boy style). Never transcribe real game melodies.
- **14 types, our own (r87, owner-approved 2026-10-20; replaced the 18 copied from Pokémon):** plain names for the elements (Fire, Water, Electric, Ice, Plant, Earth, Wind, Metal) and our own for the six that are only ours (Glitch = corrupted data, Arcane = the old world's mind, light and myth, Shade = darkness, Phantom = spirits, echoes of the dead civilization, Beast = raw instinct, Brawl = trained fighting). **Every matchup has a reason a player could guess** (`TYPE_WHY` / `TYPE_WHY_HALF` in data.js: "water puts fire out", "you can't punch a shadow"); `CHART` is built from them, 2x or 1/2 only, no immunities, a same-type hit is always 1/2. Each type hits 2-3 types hard and is weak to 2-3 (Earth hits 4 and is weak to 4, Metal is the armoured one, Plant the fragile one). **Type and look are separate:** a move's `type` decides damage, its `style` (the old type word: bug, dragon, psychic...) decides its art and sound in the shared fx.js and audio.js (`styleOf(mv)`, `fxOf(mv)`, `FX_STYLES` palettes), so every attack kept its look when the types changed. Old -> new: normal and fighting split into Beast and Brawl, grass and bug -> Plant, rock and ground -> Earth, psychic, fairy and dragon -> Arcane, dark -> Shade, ghost -> Phantom, poison -> Glitch, flying -> Wind, steel -> Metal. Relic elements use the same words (electric, metal, plant). The poison status (`psn`) keeps its name for now (the owner might want it to become Glitch's "Corrupted"). `types.html` is the design page with every reason. **Making matchups obvious (the owner's wish):** move buttons get a green ▲ (hits your target hard) or a red ▼ (barely hurts it), against whatever the move auto-aims at; a hit explains itself the first 3 times each matchup comes up (`explainMatchup`, counts in `Save.data.explained`); damage numbers are big and bright for 2x, small and grey for 1/2; every wild Coreature shows its type badges beside its health bar (`TYPE_ICON_ART`, `typeIcon`, `typeIconURL`, also in HUD type tags); the party's Arc-cores show a ▲ on anyone with a move that hits this room's wild Coreatures hard; type tooltips list every matchup with its reason.
- **Swapping mid-fight doesn't pause the game**; the swapping side is briefly open to attack, with an 8 s cooldown; HP and status carry over.
- **Beam sound:** Normal is the base sound; every other type adds a flavour layer (`BEAM_LAYERS`, levels balanced via `LAYER_GAIN`). Not every monster needs a beam.
- **Move kinds** (comment above `MOVES` in `js/data.js`): projectile, beam, dash, burst, zone, buff, heal, vortex, plus modifiers `drain`, `boomerang`, `hits` (combo dash), `linger` (pools). A new kind needs battle code, drawing, CPU use and dodging (`AI.pick` / `AI.threat`), and a sound level-checked against the others.
- **Balance lesson:** a beam roots its user while it charges and fires, so a fast monster without one gets much stronger against bots. When changing a monster's moves, re-check its stats too.
- **PC controls:** move with WASD/arrows; left click = move 1, right click = move 2, Shift + left = move 3, Shift + right = move 4 (J K L ; also work); Q swaps; Esc/P pauses; M mutes. On PC the move buttons sit in a 2×2 block shaped like the mouse. Touch has a floating stick and move buttons; gamepads use A/B/X/Y, bumpers swap, Start pauses.
- **Floating numbers are rounded** (the pixel font has no decimal point; 245.6 would read "2456").

## Testing

**Run `test.html` after every change** (`?quick` = the checks plus one run per starter, about a minute; use it for most changes and the full default for balance) (serve the folder with `python serve.py`, a no-cache version of `python -m http.server 8137`, then open http://localhost:8137/test.html; it also refreshes the browser's copies of the game files itself, so it never tests a stale build). It loads the real game in a frame, stubs `Save.write` so the owner's save is never touched, mutes, and checks: every sprite in all 5 views, every song renders at a sane level, and hard bots play full runs (10 normal + 2 endless per starter by default; `?runs=`, `&endless=`, `&maxfloor=`, `&starter=`). The bot also learns moves: it keeps heals and buffs and swaps out its weakest attack (damage per second, same-type bonus included) when the new one is clearly better; `&learn=0` turns that off for comparisons.
